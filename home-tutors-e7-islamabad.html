How to Stop Losing Marks by Misreading the Question

Many “careless” mistakes are not arithmetic slips. They are reading slips: the student answers a related question that was never asked. A short pre-answer ritual reduces that waste.

The 20-second read protocol

  1. Circle the command word: calculate, explain, compare, list, show that.
  2. Underline what must be found — the actual target, not the first number in the sentence.
  3. Box every given number and unit.
  4. Rewrite the demand in five words at the top of your working (e.g. “find acceleration in m/s²”).

Example of a classic trap

Question: “A car travels 150 m in 5 s. Calculate its average speed. State the unit.”

Misread response: student finds acceleration or writes only “30” with no unit.

Correct path: average speed = distance ÷ time = 150 ÷ 5 = 30 m/s. The command was calculate and state the unit — both parts are required.

Why this works

Under time pressure the brain jumps to familiar patterns. Forcing the command word into focus interrupts that jump. Students using home tuition in E-7 Islamabad can practise the protocol on every past-paper question until it becomes automatic.

One-week drill

For seven days, do five mixed questions. Award yourself a separate “reading mark” only if the five-word demand matches what you actually answered. Track how often that mark is lost.
