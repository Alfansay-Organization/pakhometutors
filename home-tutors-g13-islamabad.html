Percentages in Daily Life: Discounts, Scores and “How Much More?”

Percentages appear on shop signs, exam reports, and news headlines. Students who only meet them as textbook exercises often freeze when the same idea arrives in a word problem. Anchoring percentage to everyday decisions removes the freeze.

Discount: what do you actually pay?

A jacket costs Rs 4,000 with 25% off.

Discount amount = 25/100 × 4000 = 1000
Price to pay = 4000 − 1000 = Rs 3,000

Shortcut: paying 75% of the original price means 0.75 × 4000 = 3000. Same result, fewer steps once you see that “25% off” means “pay 75%”.

Exam scores: percentage is a fair comparison

Scoring 34 out of 40 looks better than 40 out of 50 until you convert:

34/40 = 85%
40/50 = 80%

The first score is stronger. Percentage puts different totals on the same scale.

“How much more?” is not the same as “what percentage more?”

If Class A has 20 students and Class B has 25, Class B has 5 more students. As a percentage of Class A: (5/20)×100 = 25% more. Always ask: percentage of which quantity?
