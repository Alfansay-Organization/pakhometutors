Knowing a Formula Is Not the Same as Knowing When to Use It

Students often memorise F = ma, v = u + at, and density = mass ÷ volume. In a mixed paper, the hard part is not recalling the symbols — it is deciding which relationship fits the story in the question.

A decision checklist

  • What is given? (numbers + units)
  • What is asked? (speed, force, energy, time…)
  • Which quantities link given to asked in one or two steps?
  • Do I need a rearrangement, a conversion, or a second formula?

Worked contrast

A cyclist speeds up from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 3 seconds. Find the acceleration.

Relevant idea: acceleration is change of velocity per time.
a = (v − u) ÷ t = (8 − 2) ÷ 3 = 2 m/s²

The same numbers in a different story (“find the force if mass is 40 kg”) would need F = ma after finding a. Choosing the formula follows the question’s demand, not the first formula you remember from the chapter.

Practice that builds selection skill

Take five questions from different chapters. For each, write only: Given / Asked / Formula name — no calculation yet. Check whether you chose the right tool before you touch the calculator.

This selection skill is exactly what mixed exams test. Learners using home tutors in F-8 Islamabad benefit when practice includes “which formula?” drills, not only substitution into a formula already named in the textbook exercise title.
