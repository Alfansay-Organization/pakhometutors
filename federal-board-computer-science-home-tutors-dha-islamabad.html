Why Computers Use Binary: Two States That Stay Reliable

Digital hardware is built from components that are easy to keep in two stable states — on/off, high/low voltage. Calling them 0 and 1 lets us encode numbers, text, and instructions as patterns of bits.

Why not ten states?

Distinguishing ten precise voltage levels reliably under noise and heat is harder than distinguishing two. Binary is chosen for robustness and simple circuitry, not because the universe is binary.

Example: the number 13 is 1101 in binary (8+4+1). Every app on your phone is ultimately huge collections of such bit patterns.
