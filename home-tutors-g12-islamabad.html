Why Checking Units Saves Marks in Science Papers

In physics and chemistry, a number without a unit is an unfinished thought. Students often calculate carefully, then lose a mark because the answer is left as “5” instead of “5 m/s”, or because cm was never converted to m.

A worked example

A car travels 1500 metres in 50 seconds. Find its average speed.

Speed = distance ÷ time = 1500 ÷ 50 = 30.

Thirty what? If distance is in metres and time in seconds, speed is 30 m/s. Write the unit in the same step as the number, not as an afterthought.

Unit analysis as a safety check

Before trusting a formula, check that the units on both sides match. For example, if you rearrange a formula and end up with seconds in the numerator where you expected metres, the algebra — not the calculator — is wrong.

Common conversion traps

  • cm³ to m³ (a factor of 1,000,000, not 100)
  • minutes to seconds before using formulas that need seconds
  • grams to kilograms in density or force calculations

Write the conversion on paper. Mental shortcuts under time pressure are where slips hide.

Exam habit

In the last thirty seconds of a numerical question, run a unit-only check: Does every measured quantity have a unit? Does the final answer unit match what the question asked for (speed, force, energy, concentration)?
