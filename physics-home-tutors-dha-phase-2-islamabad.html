If Two Objects Have Different Weights, Which Reaches the Ground First?

Intuition says the heavier object wins. In free fall without air resistance, both accelerate at the same rate g. Weight and mass scale together, so acceleration does not favour the heavier object.

The reasoning

Weight = mg. Newton’s second law: a = F/m = (mg)/m = g. Mass cancels. On the Moon, a hammer and a feather fall together.

Why experience misleads us

On Earth, air resistance matters for light, large-area objects. A feather slows; a stone does not as much. That is drag, not a failure of free-fall physics.
