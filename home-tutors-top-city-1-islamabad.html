Why Boiling Water Does Not Become Hotter Indefinitely

At boiling point, added heat goes into changing liquid water into steam (latent heat of vaporisation), not into raising temperature. Until the phase change finishes, the liquid’s temperature stays near the boiling point (at constant pressure).

A stronger flame boils water faster — more steam per minute — but does not make the liquid much hotter than 100°C at standard pressure.
