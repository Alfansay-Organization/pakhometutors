Write “Given” and “Required” First — A Habit That Clears Confused Thinking

When a question feels confusing, students often start calculating with the first number they see. Structured solvers do the opposite: they slow down for twenty seconds and sort information.

Template

Given: (facts, numbers, conditions)
Required: (exactly what must be found)
Bridge: (formula, rule, or method that connects given to required)

Example (algebra)

“The sum of two consecutive integers is 47. Find the integers.”

Given: two consecutive integers; sum = 47
Required: the two integers
Bridge: if n is the first, then n + (n+1) = 47

2n + 1 = 47 → 2n = 46 → n = 23
Integers: 23 and 24

Without the template, students sometimes introduce three variables or guess randomly. With it, the equation almost writes itself.

When the method is not obvious

Still write Given and Required. The Bridge line may stay blank for a minute — that blank is useful. It tells you which idea you must look up or recall, instead of stirring numbers hopefully.

This habit transfers across maths, physics, chemistry calculations, and even structured English answers (“Given the writer’s description… Required: explain the effect…”).
