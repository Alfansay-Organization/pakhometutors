Natural Selection Is Not Simply Survival of the Strongest

Natural selection favours traits that improve reproductive success in a given environment — not raw strength alone. A weaker but better-camouflaged individual may leave more offspring than a strong, conspicuous one.

Core ingredients

  1. Variation in the population
  2. Heredity of some of that variation
  3. Differential survival and reproduction
  4. Change in trait frequencies over generations

Selection pressure depends on the environment. What is advantageous in a drought may not be advantageous in a flood year.
