How to Revise the Night Before Without Panicking

The night before an exam is for stabilising what you already know, not for learning whole chapters. Panic revision usually means random page-flipping. A short fixed plan works better.

A calm 90-minute evening plan

  1. Minutes 1–10: List only the topics that already appeared in your weak-question log.
  2. Minutes 11–50: One mixed mini-paper or five mixed questions — cold attempt.
  3. Minutes 51–70: Mark, then rebuild only the two weakest answers.
  4. Minutes 71–85: Flip through your one-page maps or formula sheet once.
  5. Minutes 86–90: Pack bag, set alarm, stop.

Stop after ninety minutes. Sleep protects memory more than another hour of tired reading.

What not to do

  • Start a brand-new textbook chapter
  • Reread entire notes from page one
  • Compare how much others claim they studied
