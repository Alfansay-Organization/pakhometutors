How to Break a Multi-Step Exam Question into Small, Safe Steps

Long questions intimidate students because they look like one giant demand. In reality, almost every multi-mark question is a chain of smaller questions. Your job is to find the chain.

A physics-style example

“A 2 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the force. Then find the work done if it moves 4 m in the direction of the force.”

Step A: F = m a = 2 × 3 = 6 N
Step B: W = F × d = 6 × 4 = 24 J

Two short calculations; one story. Students who try to invent a single formula for the whole paragraph usually get lost.

A reliable process

  1. Underline every number and its unit.
  2. Number the tasks hidden in the wording (find force… then find work…).
  3. Solve Task 1 completely, with unit.
  4. Ask: “Does Task 2 need the answer from Task 1?” If yes, carry it forward carefully.
  5. Write a one-line final answer for each task, not only at the very end.

Why partial method marks matter

Even if the final number is wrong, clear steps often earn method marks. A blank page earns nothing. A wrong final line with correct earlier reasoning can still score.

Practise on past papers by forcing yourself to label Step 1, Step 2, Step 3 before calculating. Tutors providing home tuition in G-14 Islamabad can model this labelling out loud until it becomes automatic under time pressure.
