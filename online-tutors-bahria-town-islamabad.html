Why Passengers Move Forward When a Car Suddenly Stops

When a car brakes hard, you lurch forward. Your body was moving at the car’s speed. The car slows because the brakes act on the wheels; nothing has yet acted on you in the same way. Inertia keeps your body moving until the seatbelt (or dashboard) applies a force.

Newton’s first law in daily life

An object keeps its velocity unless a net force acts. The car’s velocity changes; yours does not, until a force does. That is why seatbelts matter: they provide the force that slows you with the vehicle.
