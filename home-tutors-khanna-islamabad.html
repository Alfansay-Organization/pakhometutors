Why Objects Float or Sink

An object floats when the upward buoyant force equals its weight. Buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced. If average density is less than the fluid’s density, it floats; if greater, it sinks.

A steel ship floats because its overall density (steel + air inside) is less than water’s, even though steel alone sinks.
