Why Mixing Easy and Hard Questions Beats Studying in Difficulty Order

Many students do all easy questions first, then leave hard ones “for later.” That builds confidence but trains the brain only on comfortable patterns. Real papers jump difficulty without warning.

Interleaving in practice

Build a sheet of eight questions: three easier, three medium, two hard — from different topics if possible. Attempt them in the mixed order printed, not rearranged by comfort.

Why the mix helps

  • You practise choosing a method under uncertainty
  • You notice when a “hard” question is only a familiar idea in new clothes
  • You reduce the shock of the first tough question in the real exam

Simple rule

If you only ever succeed when the question looks like yesterday’s homework, you have practised recognition, not transfer. Mixed sets force transfer.
