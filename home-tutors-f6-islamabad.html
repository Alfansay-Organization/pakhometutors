How to Use Past Papers Without Just Memorising the Answers

Past papers are powerful — and easy to misuse. Many students read a question, glance at the mark scheme, nod, and move on. That trains recognition, not the ability to produce an answer under exam pressure.

A better three-pass method

  1. Cold attempt: Close notes. Set a timer. Write a full answer as if it were the real exam.
  2. Mark honestly: Use the official mark scheme. Circle every mark you missed and write why (misread, weak method, incomplete explanation).
  3. Rebuild the gap: Without looking at the scheme, rewrite only the weak part. Then check again.

If you skip the cold attempt, the paper becomes a reading exercise. If you skip the rebuild, the same gap returns in the next paper.

Rotate topics, do not binge one chapter

Doing five similar algebra questions in a row feels productive. Real papers mix topics. After one paper, pull out the two weakest question types and practise those from a different year — not five more copies of the same style.

What “using the mark scheme well” looks like

Do not only check the final number. Notice command words (state, explain, show that) and how many separate points the scheme awards. Many lost marks are incomplete explanations, not wrong science.

Try this week
